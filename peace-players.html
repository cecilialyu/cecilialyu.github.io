From First Touch to Lasting Support

A full CRM strategy for PeacePlayers — a global nonprofit using basketball to unite divided communities — turning an undifferentiated list of 21,099 contacts into six segmented journeys, two lead-scoring models, and three live HubSpot dashboards.

The Problem

PeacePlayers has used basketball to unite divided communities and grow young leaders for over 25 years, reaching 150K+ participants across 25+ locations since 2001. But the organization had supporters — it just had no way to see them: thousands of contacts and donors with no signal on who was engaged, who was drifting, or who could give more.

Our central finding reframed the whole strategy: the biggest growth opportunity wasn't finding new people, it was reawakening the ones who already believed in the mission. The supporters most likely to give were already reading the emails; the donors most likely to return had never fully left.

The problem, the gap, and the fix

Sizing the Opportunity

We funneled the full database of 21,099 contacts down to the segments that actually mattered: 11,286 reachable by email, 3,065 who had ever donated, and just 167 active donors who had given in the last 365 days — split evenly between 83 first-time and 84 repeat donors.

Mapping engagement against giving history surfaced the real opportunity zone: 2,306 engaged non-donors who read every email but have never given, plus 795 lapsed donors still opening messages — both well within reach, versus 6,424 contacts who've gone quiet entirely.

Database funnel: 21,099 → 167 active donors

The Playbook — Six Segments, Six Plays

We built a segmentation framework around six supporter types, each with a clear goal and journey: On the Bench (engaged non-donors → convert), Rookies (first-time donors → retain), Sidelined (lapsed but still reading → win back), plus Starters, In the Stands, and Off the Court as lower-priority segments, with New Contacts and Store Buyers built in as future triggered journeys.

Meet Maya — A Supporter's Full Path

To make the strategy concrete, we mapped a real supporter archetype — Maya, a 30-something marketing manager in Chicago who discovers PeacePlayers at a community event — through every stage: subscribe → read without giving → first gift → second gift → lapse while still opening emails. Each stage hands off to a different automated journey.

Maya's supporter journey map

Three Journeys, Built in Depth

We went deep on the three journeys carrying the most weight. Each was grounded in supporter jobs-to-be-done research (functional, emotional, social) and cited data on donor psychology — for instance, 67.7% of engaged non-donors say trusting a charity is essential before giving, while 30% of lapsed Millennial donors said they simply didn't feel connected to the cause.

  • Engaged Non-Donors → Convert: branches by click history — a shorter direct-ask path for clickers, a longer nurture path for non-clickers, both exiting to retention on a gift
  • First-Time Donors → Retain: thank + impact email, a “get to know you” touch, then a second-gift ask, closing the biggest leak in the donor lifecycle before new donors drift away
  • Lapsed Donors → Win-Back: a two-step sequence — impact update, then a win-back ask — exiting cleanly if there's no response

The Tools We Handed Off

Strategy on a slide doesn't get built — so we shipped an interactive HTML playbook (enrollment criteria, content, A/B tests, and branching logic for all six journeys) alongside three dashboards built and live in HubSpot: an Executive Overview for leadership, Campaign Performance for the marketing team, and Journey Tracking with two automated lead scores — an Engagement Score and a Donor Value (RFM) score — that branch every contact into the right path automatically.

Cleaning the Data Underneath It All

None of the segmentation works on bad data. We identified and corrected deal-stage and gift-date validation issues that had been silently distorting lifetime giving totals and monthly pipeline reporting — then left a roadmap for collecting cleaner contact data, richer personalization signals, and properly structured gift records going forward.
